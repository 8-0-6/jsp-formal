The "287 solved problems with no Lean proof" opportunity does not exist

## How the catalog misled us

Two separate failure modes, both now confirmed by evidence:

1. **"Lean proof: No" lags reality by months.** The catalog only flips the
   field when a recording PR is merged. Meanwhile proofs accumulate publicly
   in `plby/lean-proofs`, `google-deepmind/formal-conjectures`, and solvers'
   own repos, and third parties file "[Correction]" issues on the awards repo
   registering them (issues #16, #22, #24, #44, #89, #464, #465 and more).
2. **"Elapsed years" is computed to the status-change date, not the solve
   date.** All three candidates advertise 65 to 71 years. The real histories
   are 1 to 12 years (per problem below). The number that made this band look
   valuable is a generator artifact.

## JSP-001020 = Erdős #1215 (lemniscate escape path)

**Statement.** For polynomials P with P(0)=1 and every root on the unit
circle: is there a universal constant C so that 0 can be joined to the unit
circle by a path of length at most C inside {z : |P(z)| < 1}?

1. **Resolution.** Negative (site status: disproved, set 2026-04-21). The
   answer is an explicit family of polynomials whose sublevel sets wind so
   much that every escape path is long. Attribution: G. R. MacLane,
   "On a conjecture of Erdős, Herzog, and Piranian", Michigan Math. J.
   (1953/54), pages 147-148. The site page was created ~2026-04-04 and marked
   disproved 17 days later, crediting the 71-year-old note.
2. **Real page count.** MacLane's note is 2 pages. A quantitative follow-up,
   arXiv:2606.19178 "Shortest paths in polynomial lemniscate sublevel sets
   and a problem of Erdős" (June 2026), proves growth bounds (for example
   S(n) <= pi*n); its page count could not be fetched from this environment:
   unknown. Self-containedness of MacLane's argument: classical complex
   analysis, unknown in detail.
3. **Mathlib prerequisites.** Checked concretely in a sparse mathlib4 clone:
   polynomials over C, roots, paths exist; arc length exists only as bounded
   variation (`Mathlib/Analysis/BoundedVariation.lean`); no curve-length API
   fit for lower-bounding all paths, no sublevel-set topology for
   lemniscates. The existing formalization needed five support modules
   (Barrier, Geometry, Reciprocal, Separator, Topology) on top of Mathlib.
4. **Already formalized: YES.**
   `plby/lean-proofs/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos1215.lean` + 5 modules,
   sorry-free, "Mac Lane's negative answer", formal authors Codex and
   GPT-5.6 Sol. Not in formal-conjectures (statement never written there).
   Site formal_status still "unformalized": the flag lags, the proof exists.
5. **Estimate if it had been free.** 60 to 120 h, low confidence. Lower
   bounds on the length of every path in a winding open set is delicate
   plane topology; nothing reusable in Mathlib.
6. **Verdict: REJECT.** Already formalized publicly, and the 71-year figure
   is an artifact: the problem was killed in 1953/54, roughly a year after it
   circulated and before EHP's own 1955 paper appeared. True longevity is
   about 1 year. If one instead credits the June 2026 preprint (MacLane's
   construction reportedly does not control the roots), the venue is an
   unpublished arXiv preprint. Either reading fails our tier logic, and the
   Lean race is already lost regardless.

## JSP-000402 = Erdős #501 (free sets for set mappings of small measure)

**Statement.** For every x in R let A_x be a bounded set of outer measure
less than 1. Must there be an infinite X with x not in A_y for all distinct
x, y in X? (Second question: closed A_x of measure < 1, free set of size 3.)

1. **Resolution.** The first question is **independent of ZFC**. CH gives a
   counterexample (Hechler 1972); adding continuum-plus random reals forces a
   positive answer (E. Glazer with GPT-5.6 "Sol", 2026). The second question
   is a ZFC theorem (Newelski-Pawlikowski-Seredynski 1987). The JSP catalog
   entry itself already names "Glazer, using GPT-5.6 Sol, Fable 5 and
   Opus 4.8" as proof contributors and carries a review note that "Solved"
   records independence, not a ZFC proof.
2. **Real page count.** The cited papers (Gl62 3pg, NPS87 5pg, ErHa60 5pg,
   He72 3pg) are context, not the resolution. The resolution is an
   unpublished 2026 paper plus a forcing argument. The "5 pages" that put
   this problem in our band measured the wrong papers.
3. **Mathlib prerequisites.** Mathlib has first-order model theory and ZFSet
   but **no forcing and no Boolean-valued models**. The formalization had to
   vendor a Lean 4 port of Flypitch (the CH-independence project) to state
   and prove independence. That is person-months of infrastructure.
4. **Already formalized: YES, by the resolvers.** `elliotglazer/erdos501`
   (@ 218d1c1): seven comparator-checked targets, axiom-audited, CI green,
   every line of Lean written by Claude Fable 5 and Opus 4.8 under Glazer's
   direction. Also vendored wholesale into `plby/lean-proofs` as Erdos501.
   Statement exists in formal-conjectures (501.lean).
5. **Estimate.** Not reproducible under any budget we have. Moot.
6. **Verdict: REJECT.** An independence result formalized by its own
   resolvers, already credited to them in the prize catalog. There is
   nothing here for us to claim.

## JSP-000216 = Erdős #245 (sumset growth of density-zero sets)

**Statement.** For infinite A in N with |A ∩ [1,N]| = o(N):
limsup |(A+A) ∩ [1,N]| / |A ∩ [1,N]| >= 3.

1. **Resolution.** Affirmative, by G. Freiman. The citable object is his
   monograph "Foundations of a structural theory of set addition" (1973
   translation), vii+108 pages. The constant 2 follows from Mann 1960
   ("Ma60", the 7-page paper our band table measured); the constant 3 needs
   Freiman's structure theory, in particular the 3k-4 inverse theorem plus a
   multi-scale limsup argument.
2. **Real page count.** A 108-page monograph, of which the working core is a
   chapter-scale extract plus 3k-4. Not self-contained at 7 pages. The
   catalog's "7pg" was Ma60's page range, which only gives the weaker bound.
3. **Mathlib prerequisites.** Checked concretely: Mathlib has
   Plunnecke-Ruzsa, Cauchy-Davenport, Freiman homomorphism definitions, and
   very-small-doubling lemmas. It does **not** have the 3k-4 theorem or any
   AP/GAP-structure inverse theorem. The existing formalization had to build
   them (modules: GAPTheory, Inverse, Chain, Conic, Counting, Gap, Core).
4. **Already formalized: YES.**
   `plby/lean-proofs/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos245.lean`, sorry-free,
   proving verbatim the formal-conjectures statement (245.lean there is still
   sorried; plby's is closed). Registered on the prize repo as issue #465,
   "[Correction] JSP-000216: existing Lean proof of Erdős 245 (Freiman
   sumset bound), reproduced and kernel-replayed", pinned to the same commit
   we audited (8822f7dd).
5. **Estimate if it had been free.** 80 to 160 h, medium confidence. 3k-4
   alone is a serious project; it is why this one sat unformalized.
6. **Verdict: REJECT.** Taken and registered. Also the honest longevity is
   1961 to 1973, 12 years, not 65: another elapsed-years artifact.

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Do not pursue any of the three candidates.** All rejected above, on
   evidence.
2. **Do not pick a replacement from the solved-Erdős pool at all.** The pool
   is five problems, two are taken, three are bad, and the sweep is faster
   than we are. A race against a pipeline that produced 632 formalizations
   cannot be won by choosing targets more cleverly inside its own hunting
   ground.
3. **Treat PR #969 as the gating experiment (per D3 and D10).** Zero value
   has ever been paid out by this prize. Before spending another 40-hour
   budget, wait for evidence that a submission can convert: our own review,
   or any first merge into awards.json.
4. **If we keep playing while waiting, the only under-swept ground is the
   non-Erdős part of the JSP catalog** (the 186 entries screened in
   `next-candidates.md`, of which JSP-000301 was one). plby's repo is
   organized around erdosproblems.com and does not track the JSP catalog's
   non-Erdős entries directly, though his MathOverflow, Wikipedia and arXiv
   directories overlap it. Any rescreen must now check candidates against
   his repo and the awards issue tracker first, per D15.
5. **Speed on freshly resolved problems is no longer a reliable edge
   either.** #193 went from arXiv to self-formalized on day one. Assume new
   resolutions arrive with their own Lean or get swept within days.
