## Erdős #488: CLOSED (no counterexample; conjecture looks true and sharp)

Setup: for finite A, F(x) counts x-bounded multiples of A. A witness needs
n·F(m) >= 2·m·F(n) with m > n >= max(A). Note the conjecture is a strict
inequality, so exact equality would already answer it. Searches, all with
exact integer confirmation for anything near 2:

- Exhaustive: every A of size <= 5 inside [2,13], windows to m = 6000. None.
- Every pair in [2,600], every triple in [2,130], all quadruples of
  multiples of 6 up to 240 (about 460k sets). Best: A = {599,600} at
  1.9950, below the singleton benchmark.
- Dense windows A = (N/2, N]: ratio only ~1.09.
- 4000 random sparse sets and three hill-climb runs (9000 evaluations):
  every climb strips A down to a singleton and stops there.

Finding: the extremal family is the singleton A = {a}, n = 2a-1, m = 2a,
with ratio exactly 2 - 1/a. The supremum over all instances is 2,
approached but never attained. A per-element bound shows a single element
cannot beat 2; the only theoretical route above 2 is lcm-overlap terms
subtracting from the denominator, and every configured overlap we tried
(and every optimizer trajectory) loses more in the numerator. Empirically
the conjecture is true with 2 sharp.

Consequence: the falsifiable bet on #488 is closed. The residual upside on
this problem would be PROVING the strict inequality (a real theorem, and
the sharp singleton family explains why Erdős put the 2 there), which is
original mathematics outside this ticket's scope.

## Erdős #699: CLOSED (no counterexample to n = 8.5 million)

Setup: a witness is (n, i, j), i < j <= n/2, such that no prime p >= i
divides gcd(C(n,i), C(n,j)). Big binomials never materialize: primes
p >= i dividing C(n,i) must divide n-k for some k < i (p > k kills the
denominator except p = i, which the exact test handles), and p | C(n,r)
is Kummer's carry criterion, a base-p digit comparison. Candidate j's are
enumerated from the smallest no-carry digit box and filtered by the rest.

Validation: agrees exactly with big-integer brute force over all
n <= 200, i <= 5 (both find zero counterexamples), and spot-checks match,
e.g. gcd(C(22,3), C(22,11)) = 28 with 7 as its only prime >= 3, exactly
the near-miss the searcher reports.

Results:

- n <= 30,000, i <= 5: zero hits, 7,721 near-misses (a single prime of Q
  blocking).
- n <= 1,000,000, i <= 6: **zero hits**, 409,809 near-misses. Run time
  27 minutes.
- Escalation (`e699v2.py`), n from 10^6 up, i <= 8, box cap raised to 5M
  for smooth n (the corner where a counterexample should be likeliest,
  since many small prime factors of n, n-1, n-2 make the no-carry boxes
  large): reached **n = 8,500,000 with zero hits and zero skips** before a
  container restart ended it 1.5 M short of the 10^7 target. Zero skips is
  the important number: over that whole range the search was exhaustive,
  not sampled, so this is a clean negative result rather than a partial
  one. 4 h 20 m of compute.

Reading: near-misses are common and get commoner (roughly 4.7% of (n,i)
pairs near 10^6), but the full conjunction never once fails, and the
margin shows no sign of narrowing as n grows. Erdős and Szekeres look
right. Ticket closed. Restarting to finish the last 1.5 M is not worth it:
nothing in the data suggests the first counterexample sits just past 8.5 M
rather than nowhere at all.

Caveat recorded in the screen doc stands: this genre is being actively
mined by others right now; treat any hit as race-critical and convert
immediately.

## Erdős #617 (r = 5): CLOSED, no verdict

Encoded "does K_26 admit a 5-colouring where every 6-subset sees all 5
colours" as SAT: one-hot colour per edge, plus for each colour and each of
the 230,230 six-subsets a clause demanding an edge of that colour inside.

- v1 (`e617.py`, 1,625 vars / 1,154,730 clauses, light symmetry breaking):
  CaDiCaL ran 50 minutes, no verdict.
- v2 (`e617v2.py`, 191,375 vars / 1,533,965 clauses, monotone colour
  ordering on vertex 0's star plus provable per-colour edge-count bounds
  55 <= |H_c| <= 105): ran 3 h 15 m, no verdict, killed.

Closed, for a reason that was in the screen doc and is worth restating:
**only SAT would have paid.** A satisfying assignment is a finite witness
disproving the conjecture. UNSAT, which is the expected answer given that
r = 3 and r = 4 are both proved impossible, would be a genuine new
mathematical result but is useless to us: certifying a 1.5M-clause UNSAT
in Lean means checking a DRAT proof, which our no-`native_decide` rule
makes infeasible. Three hours of a symmetry-broken CDCL instance with no
verdict says the instance is in the hard regime, so the cheap-lottery
premise no longer holds.

Operational note for future runs: `timeout` cannot interrupt pysat mid
`solve()`, because the SIGTERM is only delivered when the native CaDiCaL
call returns. Kill such runs by PID, and never `pkill -f <pattern>` when
the pattern also occurs in the killing shell's own command line (it
matches itself and kills the wrapper).


## Closing the compute adventure

Three tickets, three negative results, no witness. That was the expected
outcome and the budget was sized for it: about 9 h of the 15 h cap, no
money, and the pipeline never touched, since the rule was zero Lean before
a witness exists.

What the three runs actually establish, stated honestly:

- **#488** is not merely unrefuted, it looks true with the constant 2
  sharp, and we found the extremal family (singletons, ratio 2 - 1/a) that
  explains why Erdős wrote 2. That is a real piece of understanding, and it
  is also why no counterexample will be found by anyone.
- **#699** holds with no exceptions below 8.5 million despite hundreds of
  thousands of near-misses. A counterexample, if it exists, is not in the
  reachable range.
- **#617** is out of reach of a few CPU-hours, and its likely answer is one
  we could not certify anyway.

None of this is publishable and none of it is claimable. That is the
correct result to report, not a disappointment to dress up: the screen
predicted empty, the runs came back empty, and we stopped on schedule.

**The project now rests on one thing: the review outcome of PR #969.**
No further compute or formalization spend is justified until that moves.
